# HFpEF Navigator: clinician and researcher feedback pilot

**Purpose:** Determine whether the prototype helps users find, contextualize, and compare HFpEF clinical evidence. This is usability/research feedback, not a request for patient-specific clinical decisions.

## People to invite

Start with **three to five** willing cardiology researchers/clinicians, clinical research coordinators, and one medical trainee. Existing professional mentors at the VCU Pauley Heart Center may be appropriate initial contacts. Do not assume anyone has agreed to participate; request permission first.

## Fifteen-minute walkthrough

Ask each participant to explore the **public site** without coaching, then complete these tasks:

1. Find a trial investigating SGLT2 inhibition or another therapy relevant to HFpEF.
2. Identify the **exact enrollment LVEF criterion** and whether the trial also included HFmrEF.
3. Find the primary endpoint, comparator, effect-measure type, and source publication.
4. Compare two trials, and describe any endpoint/population differences that make direct comparison inappropriate.
5. Explain the distinction between the unverified **discovery candidates** and the smaller selected **detailed study index**.

Do not ask anyone to provide a patient's data, case details, protected health information, or treatment recommendations.

## Ask these five questions

- What is the first clinical/research question you would use this tool to answer?
- Could you find the desired study in under two minutes? What blocked you?
- Which data fields are missing or confusing (EF threshold, trial phase, follow-up, endpoints, safety, publication type)?
- Were the evidence provenance, uncertainty, and verification labels clear?
- Would you use this again? What one change would most improve it?

## Record a brief feedback log

Collect only consented, non-patient feedback: date, role category, task completion (yes/partial/no), specific navigation barriers, clarity of verification labels, requested features, and priority (critical/high/medium/low). Avoid names in the publicly shared log unless respondents expressly approve. Link any actionable changes to a GitHub issue.

A sample of 3–5 users is for early usability discovery only, not a representative clinical validation study.
